Restoration and maintenance

We never intended to be a restoration shop, but our customers insisted. David has been restoring cars for 40+ years and has won a number of national titles. Today, the opportunity to work on and revive pieces of history is probably his greatest joy.

My goal is to get you to drive your collector car.

David Fitzgerald, Owner

We finish what others started

Many times a car is restored, and either the shop gets tired of working on it or the customer gets tired of paying for it. The details that make the car enjoyable on a daily basis are left unattended.

For the last three years, David’s primary focus has been finishing everyone else’s restoration. He has special expertise in making old cars usable in the modern world.

What we do and what we send out

These go to highly skilled subcontractors whom David personally supervises.

David’s experience mostly ranges across American cars from 1921 to 1976, and we have taken on newer projects too.

Made new again without harming the original work

It is not unusual for us to take a completely restored, shiny car and gut it, then reinstall everything without damaging the original restoration.

From Denver to Pebble Beach

How one restoration became a proving ground for the care your car receives every day.

Years of work, one week to prove it

David Fitzgerald spent years restoring a 1958 Imperial Crown Limousine from the ground up. Every mechanical system was rebuilt. Every piece of chrome was rechased or replated. Every seal, gasket and hose was replaced. The car had to be better than when it left the factory.

That work earned Third in Class at the 2025 Pebble Beach Concours d’Elegance, one of the most prestigious classic car competitions in the world, where cars are judged on condition, authenticity and presentation.

The same care goes into your car

That standard of craftsmanship is not reserved for show cars. It is the standard applied to every vehicle at NGD. When we cover your car, charge its battery, or check its tires, we are looking at it with the same eye that prepared the Imperial for Pebble Beach.

If a tire is low, an oil spot appears, or a battery will not hold its charge, you hear about it before you come to pick the car up. That attention is built into storage, not billed on top of it.

A little history…

I had my first collector car before I had a driver’s license. Since then, I’ve owned in excess of 150 cars, all drivable, showable vehicles. Out of that number, I’ve performed probably 30+ complete restorations with numerous other “paint and interior” restorations. All of the cars, with the exception of the exotics, I endeavored to maintain and improve myself.

My passion has always been for mechanical things. As a franchise new car dealer for 30+ years, I was able to indulge my passion. Business would demand that I would sell off my collections at times to raise capital for business ventures. My collections encompassed muscle, exotic, and classic.

My last collection encompassed 55 automobiles, and when I sold my dealership, it was time to sell my collection. With my remaining 8 cars, I started looking around for a storage facility and was not impressed. So, now in retirement, I decided to open a car hotel with me as the head butler. Every day I get to come to work at a car museum and people pay me for it.
